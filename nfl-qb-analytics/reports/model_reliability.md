# Model reliability (Phase 5.5)

Generated by `python -m src.modeling.phase5_5_report`. Five-season NFL production of drafted QBs, nested chronological backtest: outer test classes 2012-2021 (107 QBs), five-season label embargo, every model choice made inside the development folds.

## Summary

* **Post-draft forecasts clearly beat the baselines; pre-draft totals barely do.** Under nested selection, average MAE skill vs the median is +1.6% pre-draft and +26.0% post-draft. Using one family for every fold does better (poisson +10.2% pre-draft, ridge +32.3% post-draft), but picking that family used the test classes. Phase 5 reported those hindsight numbers. Phase 5's class-bootstrap intervals say the same: quasi-Poisson vs the median pre-draft excludes zero for 1 of 10 statistics; ridge vs the median post-draft excludes zero for 10 of 10.
* **The automated choice is unstable pre-draft.** The inner rule picked the historical median in 7 of 10 pre-draft folds, including 3 of the 5 folds from 2017 on. With about 10 QBs per validation class, the inner data cannot reliably tell the quasi-Poisson model from the median. Post-draft, the rule moved from the mean-by-round baseline (early folds) to ridge and quasi-Poisson as history accumulated. With all 183 QBs the final rule picks `poisson` pre-draft and `ridge` post-draft, and v1.1.0 ships those.
* **Direct models stay the default.** The structured simulation's mean beats the nested selection procedure for 7 of 10 statistics pre-draft and 8 post-draft, because the procedure often fell back to a baseline. Against the families v1.1.0 actually ships, it is better for only 2 (pre-draft, vs poisson) and 3 (post-draft, vs ridge). No alternative demonstrably beats the direct models, so they stay the default and the simulation supplies year-by-year shapes only.
* **First rule rejected.** Choosing separately per statistic from three inner classes gave average skill -0.5% pre-draft and +26.8% post-draft. v1.1.0 makes one choice of family and one of interval method per feature set, pooled across the ten statistics. That rule was designed after seeing the first result, which is disclosed.
* **Systematic errors.** Pre-draft forecasts under-predict first-round QBs badly (games started bias -37.2) and over-predict rounds 4-7 (+4.0). Adding the pick shrinks the first-round bias to -7.1. Grouped by the *actual* outcome, the best careers are under-predicted (post-draft -22.5 starts for QBs with 1,000+ attempts). That is regression to the mean, unavoidable for an honest forecast. Grouped by the *forecast* (the fair check), post-draft biases are small (-0.3, -7.0, -1.7 for low/mid/high forecasts). Pre-draft forecasts sit low in every group (-13.7, -15.7, -3.1): median-type forecasts sit below the mean of a right-skewed outcome.

The interface labels the comps as raw-feature comps.
