# Draft eligibility audit (Phase 5.5)

Generated by `python -m src.modeling.draft_eligibility` from local data only (no API calls, no scraping).

## Summary

* The local data has **3,345 FBS passer-seasons with at least 100 attempts**. 2,160 were followed by another college season; 1,185 were the QB's last appearance in CFBD data.
* **Draft eligibility is not observed anywhere in the local data.** There is no declaration list, no transfer-portal table and no class year (`cfb_player_seasons.class_year` is empty for all 40,672 rows). Eligibility is inferred from the high-school recruiting class and the number of observed seasons.
* **538 passer-seasons stay `eligibility_unknown`.** They are excluded from the draft-probability population, and the forecast interface returns no P(drafted) for them.
* The Phase-5 population (556 final seasons) is "FBS QBs whose college career verifiably ended after at least four years". Its draft probability is not P(drafted) for every college QB, and should not be extended to underclassmen or players still in college.

| Question | Identifiable from local data? | How / why not |

### Hidden transfers to non-FBS schools

CFBD covers non-FBS teams broadly only from 2022. Among FBS passer-seasons that ended an FBS career (a final season, or a move to a non-FBS school), the share that continued at a non-FBS school shows how many "career ended" labels before 2022 may really be transfers down:

| period | fbs_career_exits | continued_at_non_fbs_school | share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| next season 2006-2021 (sparse non-FBS coverage) | 833 | 33 | 0.040 |
| next season 2022-2025 (broad non-FBS coverage) | 188 | 14 | 0.074 |

The difference between the two periods (+3.5% of exits) is a rough estimate of transfers down that the older data cannot see. Those QBs sit in the "undrafted, career ended" group. They were not drafted next spring either way, so the label is right, but they were not really prospects leaving football.

### Who the four-year rule leaves out

Drafted QBs by round and how long they had been out of high school:

| round_group | >= 4 yrs since HS | class year unknown | early entrant (<= 3 yrs since HS) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Round 1 | 11 | 27 | 22 |
| Rounds 2-3 | 19 | 26 | 3 |
| Rounds 4-7 | 45 | 68 | 4 |

Early entrants are concentrated in round 1. A population restricted to QBs with at least four college years therefore leaves out many of the strongest prospects. The draft-probability model never sees them, and its P(drafted) should not be applied to a true junior.

## How the definition changes the population and the probabilities

The college-only logistic draft model was refit under four definitions, with the same chronological backtest (test drafts 2014-2025, one-year label lag):

| definition | rows | drafted | draft_rate | backtest_rows | auc_own_population | brier_own_population |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| phase5_current (>=4 yrs: recruit class or observed seasons) | 556 | 138 | 0.248 | 384 | 0.878 | 0.115 |
| recruit_link_only (>=4 yrs since HS class) | 301 | 76 | 0.252 | 234 | 0.885 | 0.114 |
| likely_exhausted (>=5 yrs since HS or >=5 seasons) | 230 | 46 | 0.200 | 163 | 0.857 | 0.100 |
| naive_all_final_seasons (missing = undrafted) | 1022 | 229 | 0.224 | 585 | 0.917 | 0.094 |

All four, scored on the rows they share (the strictest population's test rows):

| definition | common_rows | observed_draft_rate | mean_predicted | calibration_in_the_large | auc_common | brier_common |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| phase5_current (>=4 yrs: recruit class or observed seasons) | 135 | 0.185 | 0.233 | -0.048 | 0.849 | 0.118 |
| recruit_link_only (>=4 yrs since HS class) | 135 | 0.185 | 0.199 | -0.014 | 0.860 | 0.105 |
| likely_exhausted (>=5 yrs since HS or >=5 seasons) | 135 | 0.185 | 0.214 | -0.029 | 0.847 | 0.108 |
| naive_all_final_seasons (missing = undrafted) | 135 | 0.185 | 0.207 | -0.022 | 0.828 | 0.118 |

Reading this:

* On the 135 shared test rows (observed draft rate 18.5%), mean predicted P(drafted) ranges from 19.9% to 23.3% across definitions. The current rule is the least calibrated here (23.3% predicted): it includes redshirt-junior and fourth-year exits, a group with a higher draft rate than the fifth-year QBs who dominate the shared rows.
* The **naive** definition (missing future record = undrafted, eligible exit) has the most rows (1,022) and the highest own-population AUC (0.917). That AUC is flattering: it adds many short-career and unknown-eligibility QBs who are easy to rank as undrafted. On shared rows its AUC is 0.828, against 0.849 for the current rule. Its labels are also wrong for undrafted early declarants it cannot identify.
* The **likely-exhausted** definition is cleaner but has only 230 rows (46 drafted).
* Because every definition is a heuristic, P(drafted) carries definition uncertainty on top of sampling uncertainty. The calibration-in-the-large column (observed minus mean predicted) is the size of that effect on the shared rows. Own-population AUCs are not comparable across definitions: a broader population contains more easy negatives.

## Decisions

* The Phase-5 population and model (`models/qb_draft/v1.0.0/`) are unchanged. They answer a narrow, defensible question: P(drafted | an FBS QB's college career verifiably ended after at least four years).
* `eligibility_unknown` is kept as an explicit class, and the forecast interface returns `status: unavailable` for P(drafted) outside the verified population. It never extrapolates.
* Round and pick projections are conditional on being drafted. They are available for anyone with a college profile, and are labelled "if drafted".
