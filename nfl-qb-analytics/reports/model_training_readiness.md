# Model training readiness: NFL QB career success

Generated by `python -m src.modeling.build_dataset` (dataset version 1.0.0). NFL observation ends with the 2025 regular season. No network calls; built only from data already in `data/`.

## Target

`career_success = int(nfl_starts_first_5_seasons >= 32)`. Starts are regular-season games in which the nflverse schedule names the QB as his team's starting quarterback (`home_qb_id` / `away_qb_id`), summed over NFL seasons `draft_year` through `draft_year + 4`. The schedule file lists a starting QB for every regular-season game from 2014 through 2025 (256 or 272 games a season; 271 in 2022, when one game was cancelled), so starts come from real game records. Games played and pass attempts are not used as a proxy.

* A QB whose five-season window is incomplete gets a value only if he has already reached 32 starts. Otherwise the target is NA (censored), never 0.
* A drafted QB with no NFL stat record counts as 0 starts only when the draft board's own career fields confirm he never played. Otherwise the target is NA.
* Cross-check against the Phase-3 outcome table (`nfl_targets`): 0 start-total mismatches in 270 rows, and 0 label mismatches in 275 rows.

## Eligible draft classes

The 2017-2021 classes are eligible: their final college season (2016-2020) is inside the play-by-play data, and their five-season NFL windows (through 2025 at the latest) are complete. 2022-2026 classes have incomplete windows. Of their 58 QBs, 5 have already reached 32 starts. The other 53 are censored (NA), not failures. These classes are scored by `predict.py` but cannot be evaluated yet.

Training support is FBS only. Every cohort QB played his final college season in FBS, because CFBD play-by-play for 2016-2020 does not cover FCS-only games. By the 2024-2025 seasons CFBD play-by-play does include FCS teams, so a few recent FCS QBs (North Dakota St., Montana St.) have play-type profiles. `predict.py` does not score them by default: their rates against FCS defenses are outside anything the model was trained on.

"NFL linked" counts QBs with an NFL season grid row. The 2026 class has none because no NFL season has been played since that draft. 2020 shows 12 of 13 because Cole McDonald is a verified never-played (0 starts), not a linked record.

## Statistical feasibility

* Sample: 53 QBs, 19 positive and 34 negative (about 36% positive), across 5 draft classes.
* Candidate features: 16 in the largest model (6 basic, 5 context, 5 play-type). That is about 1.2 positive events per feature, far below the usual 10-20 for unpenalised logistic regression. Only strongly regularised models are defensible. Gradient boosting is run only as a labelled exploratory comparison, and XGBoost is not justified at this size.
* Chronological holdout: rolling-origin by draft class. Classes 2017-2018 are the first training window, then 2019, 2020 and 2021 are each predicted from the classes before them. Cohort QBs per class:

  * 2017: 10 QBs, 3 positive, 7 negative
  * 2018: 12 QBs, 4 positive, 8 negative
  * 2019: 10 QBs, 3 positive, 7 negative
  * 2020: 12 QBs, 5 positive, 7 negative
  * 2021: 9 QBs, 4 positive, 5 negative

Every class, including each of the three holdout classes, contains both outcome classes. Pooled, the out-of-sample set is the 2019-2021 cohort QBs. With roughly 30 holdout QBs, a ROC-AUC confidence interval is about +/-0.15 wide, so only large differences between models are detectable.
* Supplementary tasks to reduce small-sample risk: (1) a shorter horizon, 16+ starts in the first 3 seasons, for classes 2017-2023 (75 QBs, 29 positive); (2) adding the 2005-2016 classes (137 QBs, 34 positive, no play-type data) to training for the A/B feature sets, to show how much sample size alone matters.
